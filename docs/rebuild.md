# Cutalong: the rebuild

Chop Shop is becoming **Cutalong**, a video editor people talk to. This page is the map: what was built, how it fits together, what runs where, and what comes next. The design system is published at https://claude.ai/artifact/AsZpkLeXa7pLaqMZkipqLh; the UI rules live in `.claude/skills/cutalong-ui/SKILL.md`.

## Principles

- **The edit is data.** Every change to a video is an op on one document. The chat, presets, notes, the script view and the beat strip all speak the same ops, so every version can be replayed, compared or undone, and a model's proposal can be checked before anything moves.
- **Timing is anchored to words**, not seconds, and the words are tightened to their sound. Strikes, tightening and re-transcription move the cut with the speech, and cuts land a hair before an onset.
- **Local first.** Processing and storage happen on the owner's computer: transcription (whisper.cpp), listening, measuring and rendering (ffmpeg), projects, assets, presets and the brand. Only two things use the network: Claude, for turning words into edits and, at export, writing titles, a description's opening and tags (behind a switch in Settings), and fetching a video from a link. Beyond those, other AI services sit behind one switch in Settings, on by default: nothing goes to one until its key is added, then only the sound (to Deepgram or AssemblyAI, to hear every um and false start) or short excerpts (to Google Gemini, to judge a take), and turning it off stops them all except the one request that deletes what AssemblyAI still holds from a job.
- **Talk, don't configure.** Settings are sentences and slash commands. Numbers live behind Details.
- **Claude reads what is said.** A word, a sentence or a whole prompt goes to Claude, which answers with a plan of edit ops and Cutalong's own commands; nothing guesses at words with patterns. Slash commands stay instant and local. So nothing runs until an Anthropic key works: Start shows the steps to one until then, and anything stopped says so in one sentence.
- **Pattern, not footage.** A video someone likes is measured for its pattern only. Material pulled into an edit (B-roll, excerpts, icons) is short, cut to for what it shows, and credited in every export.

## Phone

`mobile/` is Cutalong on iPhone and Android, editing on the phone itself: a Capacitor app that shows the same React app as the Mac and runs the same main-process work. That work reaches its machine only through `src/main/platform.ts`, which the Mac implements with Node and Electron and the phone with the `CutalongEngine` plugin and Capacitor's Filesystem, Preferences and secure storage. On the phone, ffmpeg, ffprobe and whisper.cpp run in-process with exactly the arguments their command-line tools take on the Mac, so the renderer of record, intake and transcription are one copy.

- **Phase 1:** picking clips with +, sorting them, hearing the words on the phone (the model is downloaded on first use), v1, the thread and the editor, the presets and editing commands, requests to Claude with the person's key, other AI services behind their switch, export with progress to the chat and to Photos, projects, and Settings.
- **The key gate is the desktop's** (`src/shared/setup/`): without an Anthropic key that works, Start is the steps to get one, with a button for each page and the cost line, and anything asked for says so and offers them; a key Anthropic accepted before keeps the gate open while it is busy, down or out of reach. The key is kept in the Keychain or the Android Keystore, and what is remembered of it, a fingerprint, in the app's settings, which the shared work writes through the app's own copy (`mobile/src/host/settings.ts`).
- **What differs is the host's:** the phone's hardware H.264 encoder at set bitrates in NV12; camera moves with scale and crop, as the phone's LGPL ffmpeg has no `perspective`; ffmpeg's raw output written to segment files and read back; keys in the Keychain or the Android Keystore; the screen kept on while an export runs; and the words that name the device, which the phone's page builds from `mobile/src/shims/platform.ts` in place of `src/shared/platform.ts`: this phone, its Keychain or Keystore, Photos or Files.
- **On the Mac for now,** each said in one sentence where it is asked for: vlogs, `/clipping`, `/premiere`, videos from links and B-roll search, video cards, motion graphics (chrome lettering among them), scene effects and the hype edit, grids of several videos, maps and icons, learning a brand's look and its habits from videos, links and edits, a second camera, following the speaker and LUT files. They need the Mac's yt-dlp, Vision, face finder or Skia; the export refuses a cut that has any of them before it starts. Boxed captions, which libass draws, work on the phone. Planning with Ollama is not offered.
- **What the phone sends:** the page reaches only itself and Anthropic (its content security policy); the other AI services are reached by the phone's own HTTP through the Mac's gate; the transcription model comes from whisper.cpp's published models.
- **What it carries:** Settings ends with Licences on a phone: FFmpeg, FriBidi and the engine's fftools under the LGPL, with where their source is and how to replace them, and the rest of what the app is built with.
- **Built and run in CI** (`.github/workflows/mobile-engine.yml`): the engines, then a debug APK and a simulator build, and on an emulator and a simulator the app's own flow, with a screenshot at each step: Start without a key, a key Anthropic turns down and one it takes, then picked footage to an export measured on the runner, with the text boxes on Start, in the thread and in the editor's note box tried with the keyboard up, at rest and grown to six lines, and Back from the note box; then the same project after dark with reduced motion and Anthropic down: a change asked for in the thread while the gate stays open, Start, the editor opened from Start's card with its first frame showing, the thread with the crew at an export, the Crew page and Brand. Anthropic is stood in for inside the app while a drive runs (`mobile/src/host/standin.ts`), so nothing leaves the emulator or the simulator. Store builds go to TestFlight and Google Play's internal track when their secrets are set.
- **Ready for the stores, without their secrets:** the icons, drawn from the Mac's mark at every size iOS and Android ask for (`mobile/scripts/make-icons.py`); the app's privacy manifest, which CI checks against the APIs the built app's binaries really use (`mobile/ci/privacy-apis.py`); and export compliance answered in `Info.plist`, as the app's only encryption is the system's. The listings wait.

`mobile/README.md` says how to run it on this computer through the dev bridge, what the native builds need, what CI keeps, and the store secrets.

## How an edit moves

1. **Footage in.** `cut:prepare` probes the file, transcribes it (or reuses the clip finder's transcript), listens to the audio to tighten every word, watches the speaker's mouth (lines the sound up with it, times cuts to it, marks voices from off camera), splits speech into beats, and makes v1 in the chat's brand (the default, or the one its header was switched to). The brand goes on as v2 when it is set to, and whatever its never list rules out stays off from then on.
0. **The words first.** A message sent with footage, a link, or before there is an edit is read by Claude (`cut:triage`) for the road it takes: one edit, a vlog, clips from a long recording, a question, or words kept until the footage comes.
2. **Changes.** A preset runs `planStyle`; words, a whole prompt or a set of notes go to the planner, whose plan runs in order as one version (`carry.ts`), and the words sent with the footage shape v1 itself; the script view strikes words; assets land on beats. All of them commit ops as a new version with a one-sentence summary. A stretch picked on the beat strip (`shared/edit/range.ts`) scopes them: commands act on its beats, a note names it to the planner, and `opsInside` leaves out any op that reaches past it. A length (`shared/raw/length.ts`) is cut locally from the takes, the audio map and the idea starts.
3. **Preview.** The editor plays the resolved cut straight off the recording, seeking between pieces, with punch-ins, black and white, big words and cutaways drawn live. Captions are drawn as libass burns them into the export: the same words and cues, laid out at the export's size by libass's rules (the face's cell for the size, no kerning, its wrapping and boxes) and scaled to the frame, so a caption sits within a pixel or two of where the export has it (`main/subtitles-render.test.ts` checks the drawing against libass on every ffmpeg). A phone clip in a wide edit, a still or a screen recording sits whole on a blurred copy of itself (`shared/edit/fill.ts`), drawn from a small canvas in the preview and from a small blurred copy scaled up in ffmpeg, to the same numbers; wide footage in a vertical edit fills the frame. A new edit says its fill; one saved before there was a fill says none and keeps its crop, and a version's ops never carry the fill, so 0.2.13 still opens what this build saves.
4. **Export.** `cut:render` renders the document with ffmpeg and writes beside the video a credits file when outside material is used, and what the upload needs: captions as .srt and .vtt, a description, and titles and tags. No file beside an earlier export is ever written over. The export is BT.709 and tagged so, as QuickTime, Chrome and the preview show HD: the picture passes through untouched, and every step into or out of RGB (the look, graphics, the logo, stills, a card's ground) says BT.709 and goes through planar RGB at the picture's own size, which ffmpeg 6.0, 6.1 and 7.0 all convert exactly (`main/colour-render.test.ts`). Captions go straight onto the picture, as the RGB round trip made the caption pass ten times the work: 7.0 colours them by the picture's tag, and on 6.0 and 6.1, which colour them with BT.601 (`captionMatrix` asks each ffmpeg once), their colours are moved to the ones whose BT.601 values are BT.709's. A colour BT.601 cannot name, such as pure red, is burned in RGB instead.

## Working on it

- `npm test` runs the unit tests and real ffmpeg renders and measurements on clips the tests make themselves (with `FFMPEG_PATH`, `ffmpeg-static`, or the `ffmpeg` on the PATH).
- The Mac app ships ffmpeg 6.0: `ffmpeg-static`'s newest Mac build, older than its Linux one. 6.0 has no `cover` transitions, ignores atempo's tempo command, and repeats a frame when xfade runs over long pictures, so the renderer avoids all three. Run the render tests against 6.0 as well (`FFMPEG_PATH=… npm test`, with the Linux build from the `ffmpeg-static` b6.0 release); the Mac test build runs them on its own copy.
- `npm run typecheck` checks both processes and that every IPC channel the preload uses has a handler.
- The renderer runs in a plain browser with a mock of the main process (`cutalong/mock.ts`) that answers with the real shared logic. Build with `npx electron-vite build`, serve `out/renderer`, and drive it with Playwright to see screens without Electron. It starts on the key steps: paste any key that starts `sk-ant-`, or one ending in a state (`sk-ant-test-no-credit`, `-refused`, `-busy`, `-down`, `-offline`, `-no-access`) to see that answer.
- The real app without the network: point it at a stand-in for the Anthropic API with `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL`, and give it a key in `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (read before the keychain) or paste one on Start. The stand-in answers the key check (`GET /v1/models/…`) and `POST /v1/messages` with JSON in the shape the request's schema asks for, told apart by the system prompt. On Linux with no keyring, pass `--password-store=basic` and call `safeStorage.setUsePlainTextEncryption(true)` before a key is saved.

### How a command adds itself

- **Every slash command is already a step Claude can choose.** The planner is sent the slash menu (`<commands>`: each command with its summary) and runs one with `{"op":"run","command":"music","rest":"lofi","note":""}`, through the same dispatch as typing `/music lofi`, never-list check included. A new command in the menu needs nothing more; its summary, with an example, is what teaches it. Name it in `ACTION_COMMANDS` (or as a preset, a style or a motion command) and it is in the menu.
- **A command whose arguments deserve a shape of their own** goes in `PLAN_COMMANDS` (`src/shared/plan/commands.ts`): its op name, one line that teaches it with a step that parses, and its slots, every one required (`''`, `0`, `[]` or `false` for the usual), because structured output allows few optional fields. The plan's schema, the planner's instructions and `PlannedCommand` are made from that list. Then add its check to `commandProblem` (`src/main/planner.ts`) if a step can be wrong for the cut, its executor to `CommandRunners` and `runCommandStep` (`carry.ts`; TypeScript names the missing case), and its runner in `useCutalong`'s `runners()`, reusing what its slash command calls. `planner-prompt.test.ts` checks that its example parses, and `long-prompts.test.ts` that some prompt reaches it.

## Status

Built and tested: the edit document, presets and slash commands, inspiration measuring, the renderer of record, the planner, assets (files, image links, video excerpts, B-roll search, icons, credits), listening for snappy cuts, the brand with learning, chats saved with their projects (an edit an older build could not read, and left out, comes back from the copy it kept beside the project), Settings (the Claude key, checked as it is saved; the transcription model; the export folder; video sign-in; updates), other AI services behind one switch (transcription that keeps every um, judging takes from their sound and picture), planning edits on a local model through Ollama, raw footage (takes, verbatim transcription, intake that reads and fixes each recording, the audio map, cutting profiles by kind of video), learning from the creator's own edits and chats, calm motion throughout, and the Cutalong shell. The editing skills: transitions, J and L cuts, speed, ramps, freeze and reverse, camera moves and `/focus`, a music bed that dips under speech and cuts on its beat, voice polish, sound effects, looks and LUTs, chapters, a second camera, following the speaker, and `/premiere`; and motion graphics from slash commands. Scene effects for gaming and commentary edits (a pixel scan, an impact, speed lines, a background blur, chrome lettering and the `/hype` style), in words, by command, in the preview and in the export, whose picture work matches the preview's to the bit on ffmpeg 6.0, 6.1 and 7.0 (a wide blur by one level, rarely). Vlogs: a day of clips told as a story, with asks for a length, an opening or more of a part as new versions, and the next day's clips joining it. `/clipping`: many shorts from one long stream, service or podcast, each its own edit, exported together. In the editor, a stretch picked on the beat strip for a note or a command, any edit cut to a length or without its intro, and a phone clip in a wide video, a still or a screen recording shown whole on a blurred copy of itself. A brand per channel, switched from a chat's header as a version, each with a never list said in chats or set on the Brand page (respected by `/all`, commands and the planner) and B-roll sources to search first or never use; every export comes with its upload kit. Each plays in the preview from the source files and exports through the renderer of record, and the render tests pass on ffmpeg 6.0, 6.1 and 7.0. An edit takes any number of files: they are measured and sorted into talking, better sound and B-roll, the talking clips become one edit, the better sound is lined up under them by ear, and the planner places the B-roll and asks for more where the edit wants it. The editor plays files from a local HTTP server, because Chromium's player does not resume byte ranges through Electron's protocol bridge. The classic tools are gone.

Not yet: vlog and clipping thresholds checked on real phone footage and real multi-hour recordings (both were built on planted footage, with Claude and Ollama tested on recorded answers), titles and tags from Claude read on real answers (the request is tested, the writing only on made-up ones), searching inside a preferred website rather than only moving its results up, B-roll audio, picture-only transitions where the sound runs straight through (today a transition needs a pause or spare footage either side of the cut), a second camera found at intake rather than added with `/angle`, following the speaker off a Mac (it needs `chopshop-vision`), cut-outs for the scene effects checked on a Mac (Vision's person segmentation and foreground instance masks are written and wired, but only shapes have stood in for them here), and a renamed app id. The window says Cutalong, but `appId`, `productName` and the update feed still say Chop Shop on purpose: renaming them breaks auto-update for existing installs, and the name needs a trademark and domain check first.

## Next

1. **Skills from research.** `docs/research/` has the observations, a ranked skills catalogue and a three-wave build order. Wave 1, which fits the ffmpeg renderer, is built; wave 2 needs the compositor.
2. **The compositor.** WebGL with WebCodecs, so preview equals export and keyframed motion work (kinetic type, transitions, tracking, masks) becomes possible.
3. **Automations.** A rail item for recurring jobs that run on the owner's computer: "when a video lands in this folder or a livestream ends, find the best moments, cut them as Shorts in my brand, caption them, and queue them for me." `/clipping` already does the finding and cutting; an automation runs it without being asked. Runs land in a review queue by default; posting is a separate opt-in; each run has a spending cap on Claude calls; outside material is always credited.
4. **Strategy chats.** Talk through what to make next, grounded in the channel's own numbers (read-only, with the owner's sign-in), the brand, and what has performed. The result is a plan that can start edits or automations.
5. **Lip landmarks, and calibrating the mouth on real footage.** Watching the mouth runs on ffmpeg alone and was measured on synthetic takes only. On a Mac with Xcode's command-line tools:
   - Give `chopshop-vision` a `--mouth` mode: `VNDetectFaceLandmarksRequest` on frames read in order with `AVAssetReader` (not the image generator, which is slow at full frame rate), only around pauses and over a minute of speech. Per face it adds the inner-lip opening (the gap between the inner lips' top and bottom midpoints over the distance between the eyes) and `yaw`, `roll` and `pitch` (pitch needs macOS 12). The new fields are optional, so `detectFaces` and `buildTrack` keep working.
   - Feed the lip opening to `series.ts` in place of the thumbnail openness, and mark frames with `yaw` over about 45 degrees unreadable. The offset and cut timing need no other change.
   - Measure `naturalLeadSec` once, on a take recorded with the camera's own microphone, so the lips' natural lead over the voice is not read as latency.
   - Try `-hwaccel videotoolbox` for the crop pass; decoding is its main cost, and it could not be tried off a Mac.
