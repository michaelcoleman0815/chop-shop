# Podcasts: how the most-watched ones are edited

The study behind editing podcasts in Cutalong: full episodes with a strong hook, and clips for Shorts, Reels and
TikTok. Made on 22 November 2026.

**In short:** the craft is in the first minute and in the cameras. A full episode opens on its best moments (or one
strong one), runs a short sting, and is talking within about 45 s; after that the body is left as it was said,
the only edits being camera switches that follow who is talking, about one every 10 s. Nobody zooms in an episode.
A clip opens on a question or a claim with its payoff inside 3 s, cuts to whoever is talking every 1.3 to 3 s, and
carries big short captions and, on the big shows, a headline card. Text mostly just appears; nothing bounces.

## What it rests on

- **Full episodes: 21 of the most-viewed recent video podcasts**, 58K to 6.7M views: The Diary of a CEO, Rotten
  Mango, Flagrant, Theo Von, Club Shay Shay, Call Her Daddy, Lex Fridman, JRE, Shawn Ryan, All-In, Jay Shetty,
  Impaulsive, Huberman Lab, Colin & Samir (two), Modern Wisdom, Mel Robbins, New Heights, Bobbi Althoff, My First
  Million and Rich Roll. Solo, two-person, three or more, and remote. The first 45 to 150 s of each stepped through
  every 0.1 to 0.2 s, one 2-minute window of the body at about 40% of the way through, every apparent jump cut
  checked frame by frame, every cut tested for a zoomed crop and every shot for a slow push, bursts of frames around
  each piece of text, YouTube's storyboards for the whole episode and the opening transcripts. Music and pauses were
  measured on The Diary of a CEO and Modern Wisdom only; sound effects could not be judged. One body window a show
  is a small sample.
- **Clips: 37 of the most-viewed podcast Shorts**, from Flagrant, The Diary of a CEO, Theo Von, Club Shay Shay,
  Impaulsive, Call Her Daddy, Shawn Ryan, Modern Wisdom, Bobbi Althoff, whatever, Jay Shetty and five Rogan fan
  channels; 745K to 59M views. 29 have two or more people on screen.
- **How:** the research tools were out of quota, so each Short was paused in a browser and its frames read off at
  set times: 10 across the first 3 s, 10 across the rest, every 33 ms for 1 to 1.5 s on 11 clips to time the text,
  and about 13 frames a second through 8 whole clips to count cuts and measure zooms. Nothing was downloaded.
- **Limits:** no sound was heard, so music, sound effects and words said without a caption are unknown. Only
  YouTube Shorts; no TikTok or Reels.

## Clips

### The hook

- **A question first, its answer within 3 s: 17 of 37** (19 counting two uncaptioned). The clip starts on the
  question's first word; often the asker's voice plays over the answerer's face. "Are you scared to leave the
  country?" "Yeah! I cannot go to countries like Malaysia…" (Flagrant). "Have you heard of the Bangladeshi bank
  heist?" "No." "Now we're talking." (Modern Wisdom).
- **The claim or payoff first: 5. A story's first concrete sentence: 5.** Stakes or a teaser: 4.
- **A headline card over the set-up: 11** (The Diary of a CEO, Club Shay Shay and Shawn Ryan on every clip):
  "HOW TO SPOT A PSYCHOPATH IN 5 SECONDS", "BILLIONAIRE INVESTOR: SELL BEFORE IT'S TOO LATE!", "[NAME] ON [TOPIC]".
  On from the first frame, held 1.6 to 3 s, then wiped off, glitched off or cut away.
- **No lead-in:** 31 of 37 start on a sentence's first word, the first caption up within 100 ms.
- How the opening is chosen: find the payoff, go back to the question or claim that sets it up, start on its first
  word, and keep the payoff inside the first 3 to 5 s.

### Layout and cutting

- **Full frame on whoever talks, cutting (never panning) to the other and to the listener's reactions: the main
  layout in 27 of 37.** A shot lasts 1.3 to 2.9 s on average (21 to 45 cuts a minute; Flagrant 26 in 35 s).
- **Stacked top and bottom: used in 11, the main layout in 6.** The speaker over the footage being talked about
  (Impaulsive, with a thin divider), or the host over the guest with the title in the seam (The Diary of a CEO).

### Captions and text

- Burned in on 30 of 37; 1 to 3 words a screen on 24 of those; capitals on 19. Bold condensed sans, white with a
  black edge or shadow, 40 to 62% of the way down, never at the bottom. Coloured by keyword or by speaker on 8
  (Flagrant: the asker orange, the guest white).
- **No karaoke highlight of the word being said on any.** On 8 of the 11 timed, each group of words simply replaces
  the last on one frame, every 170 to 500 ms, with no scale, fade or bounce. The Diary of a CEO's 2026 clips are the
  exception: words revealed left to right 33 to 66 ms apart, figures sliding up over about 200 ms, a black mask
  wiping the title off over about 370 ms, bold sans with italic serif.

### Zooms

- **Rarer than expected: none at all in 4 of 7 measured.** Where there are some, a slow push on the face of 1.1 to
  1.3x over 1 to 3 s, easing out, or a snap of 1.2 to 1.36x at a cut. They hold until the next cut. One fan channel
  zooms on 7 of 12 shots, alternating in and out. About 2.5 a minute on The Diary of a CEO.

### Graphics

- **A picture or footage of what is named, cut in on the word: 17 of 37,** held 0.6 to 3 s, full frame or in the
  top half. An emoji, flag or picture popping in on its word: 7 (Flagrant's flag, 45% of the width, about 315 ms
  after "COUNTRY?", gone with the next cut). A logo or handle: about 12. Name tags: 2. Progress bars: none.

### Length and endings

- Median 49 s (12 under 30 s, 19 of 30 to 60 s, 6 longer; The Diary of a CEO's run 103 to 141 s). They end on the
  punchline (10), a plain statement (13), a laugh (7), or mid-sentence so the loop is seamless (4). None ask for a
  part 2 or a subscribe.

## Full episodes

### The opening

- **How they open:** a montage of 3 or more moments from later on, 5 of 21 (The Diary of a CEO, Club Shay Shay,
  Mel Robbins, Colin & Samir, Impaulsive); one teaser moment, 5 (Huberman's single 33 s answer, Theo Von's single
  line, My First Million's 5 s, a 45 s bit on New Heights, a 35 s bit on Bobbi Althoff); a scripted intro or voiceover
  as the hook, 5 (Flagrant's roast to camera, Lex Fridman's "The following is a conversation with…", Rich Roll's
  2 to 3 minute essay over b-roll and music, Colin & Samir's voiceover, Jay Shetty's "what will people learn today?");
  no tease at all, 6 (Call Her Daddy, Shawn Ryan and JRE go sting then straight in; Modern Wisdom starts on a strong
  stretch already under way).
- **A title sting: 12 of 21,** usually 2 to 6 s, right after the tease (0:08 to 0:45), or first when there is none.
- **Talking by:** a median 45 s in; at once on Modern Wisdom, Rotten Mango and All-In; 2:00 on The Diary of a CEO.

**The fullest cold open, The Diary of a CEO's (0:00 to 2:00):**

1. The guest's most shocking claim, with no context, as the first words: "They ran what's called a honeypot
   operation against the CEO of Nvidia."
2. Question and answer in turn: 12 or 13 bites in 93 s, 3 to 20 s each, most 4 to 6 s, a one-line question from the
   host setting up each answer.
3. Rising: the premise, then credentials and numbers ("FBI estimates 10 to 15,000…"), then the personal and taboo.
4. It ends on a question left unanswered ("Have you ever killed anybody?" "Um.") and 0.2 s of black.
5. 77 cuts in 93 s (a median shot of 1.4 s): re-enacted b-roll, a name card the first time each guest is seen, single
   keywords popping on with the words, white flash frames on 4 cuts, and music under all of it (the sound never falls
   below -27 dB, against -80 to -100 dB between words in the body), swelling at 1:12.
6. Then the host to camera (21 s, asking for a subscribe), a 4 s logo sting, and the conversation at 2:00.

Other ways: one answer with an "IN THIS EPISODE" bug, then 5.6 s of quick black-and-white b-roll and the logo
(Huberman); the host's voice making the case for the guest, with short bites and the evidence on screen (Colin &
Samir, Mel Robbins); a comic bit (New Heights, Bobbi Althoff); or no tease, starting on the best stretch (Modern
Wisdom).

### The body

- **Left as it was said, but for camera switches: 18 of 21.** Every apparent jump cut in 11 shows was a gesture,
  not a cut. Only The Diary of a CEO cut time out on screen: 2 jump cuts in 4.3 minutes, mid-sentence, on the guest's
  single shot. (Time taken out under a camera switch can't be seen.) Pauses stay: none of 1.5 s or more in 4.3
  minutes of The Diary of a CEO, two on Modern Wisdom (the longest 2.5 s); both sound natural, the mics gated
  between words rather than the silences cut.
- **Camera switches: a median of 6 a minute, one about every 10 s.** Slowest: Modern Wisdom 1.5 (shots up to 66 s),
  Huberman 2, Rich Roll 2.5. Fastest: Bobbi Althoff 17.5 and Call Her Daddy 16 (a median shot of about 2 s), Flagrant
  and Colin & Samir about 10.
- **They follow the speaker, loosely.** On The Diary of a CEO, 14 of 21 turns got a switch from 1.3 s before to 0.8 s
  after the new voice; 8 of 18 switches came mid-turn, 1 to 3 s on the host listening. On Modern Wisdom 8 of 17 turns
  got one. Shots of the listener are common on Call Her Daddy, Bobbi Althoff, Theo Von and Colin & Samir.

### Zooms

- **None.** Not one punch-in, slow push or crop faking a second angle in any body window or cold open of the 21
  (the detector caught a 1.3x crop and a 4% push in a test). The only zoom-like moves: Flagrant's camera operators
  reframing live, a slow push on one photo on Lex Fridman, and zooms inside graphics (The Diary of a CEO's name card
  scaling up 2.5x in 100 ms with motion blur, My First Million zoom-blurring into a word, Colin & Samir pushing into a
  screenshot over about 800 ms).

### Graphics

- **Evidence on screen: 9 of 21.** All-In: a white article or press release full screen on black, the speaker in a
  box bottom-right, held 8 s or more. Lex Fridman: archive photos with names, a map, a clip captioned bottom-left.
  Rotten Mango: news clips labelled "SOURCE: NBC", and a strip of three vertical clips added one every 1.3 s. Colin &
  Samir: screenshots and charts (below).
- **Names on screen: 8 of 21** (a neon card on The Diary of a CEO, a lower third held 3.6 s or more on Shawn Ryan,
  gold nameplates under each guest on Club Shay Shay leaving 0.4 s apart, small standing tags on My First Million and
  New Heights). Most shows have none.
- **A corner logo: 7 of 21. Captions: none in any episode body,** only The Diary of a CEO's cold open. A subscribe
  button: Jay Shetty, animated for 4.75 s. **Chapters: 15 of 21,** sponsors marked as chapters on The Diary of a CEO
  ("Ads") and Huberman ("Sponsor"), with logos on screen on Club Shay Shay and New Heights; the hosts read them.

### Text animation (about 25 pieces timed)

- **On and off with a cut: about 10, the most common.** The Diary of a CEO's keywords appear one word at a time
  exactly on the spoken word ("basically", then "yes" 300 ms later).
- **A fade: 5** (200 to 800 ms in, about 330 ms out). **A slide or wipe: 5** (My First Million's skewed gold slab
  across in 200 ms; Colin & Samir's card up in about 350 ms and a yellow highlighter across "39.8M views" in about
  600 ms). **Letters or lines one after another: 2** (about 70 ms and 100 ms apart). **Typed with a cursor: 1**
  (about 70 ms a character). **A glitch: 1 or 2. An ink-splash logo: 1. Text behind the subject: 1.** A chart
  drawing on left to right in 1.6 s; a leaderboard swapping rows in about 400 ms.
- **No bouncy scale-pop with overshoot anywhere.**

### Sound

- YouTube's own measure puts them at a median -16.4 LUFS (from -13.6 on Call Her Daddy and All-In to -22.8 on JRE).
  Music under the cold open and the sting, and in eight shows' openings by the transcripts; none under the talk in
  either body measured. Voices gated to near silence between words.

### Colin & Samir

- **The hook is a short documentary** (37 to 53 s): the host's voice makes the case for the guest ("39.8M views",
  "#1 podcast in India") over evidence on a white page: YouTube cards with the number highlighted in yellow, a ranking
  of channels re-ordering itself, a line chart drawing on, and the guests walking into the studio. Then a 6 s sting: a
  grid of yellow circles slides for 1.1 s, breaks into a halftone circle, and the four-line wordmark resolves.
- **In the body** the guests hold up printed charts, and what they talk about (thumbnails with red arrows, clips) is
  cut in. About 10 switches a minute; no zooms, no jump cuts.
- **Their Shorts, the same template all three times:** the speaker in 9:16 mid-thought from the first frame; a hook
  title in bold black on yellow boxes ("Aim for Only / 85% Perfection") over the chest, about 45% down, for 2 to 4.5 s;
  then small white captions of 1 to 3 words about 60% down; a tiny wordmark top-left; a cut to the listener at
  about 2 s; cutaways; 39 to 53 s.

### Titles

- The Diary of a CEO: "Guest-role: shocking first-person quote!", thumbnails of 2 to 4 huge words and faces. Others:
  "Name: topic, topic & topic" (Call Her Daddy, Lex Fridman), capital keywords (Impaulsive, My First Million), plain
  numbers (JRE, Theo Von), a short claim (Colin & Samir).

## Speakers

Cutting to whoever talks needs to know who talks. Cutalong now finds it from the voices, on the computer
(`src/shared/speakers`, `src/main/speakers.ts`): the transcript is cut wherever someone new could come in, each
stretch's voice is fingerprinted by 3D-Speaker's CAM++ through onnxruntime-node, and the fingerprints are grouped
into speakers. On a conversation in three of the Mac's voices it named all 223 words right, a one-word "Right." too,
in 1.4 s for 75 s of sound. On 6 minutes of Modern Wisdom (Chris Williamson and Alex O'Connor, two young British
men) it found the two of them and matched who the camera showed at nearly every 4 s mark, including Alex's 6 s
interruption. Two Mac voices made by the same engine at different pitches it could not tell apart (the model
scores them more alike than one of them against himself).

## What Cutalong needs

From both studies, most difference first. What Cutalong has already: a podcast clip finder (30 to 90 s, keeps
laughs, knows an answer can need its question), a podcast cut that leaves pauses and ums, captions and their styles,
lower thirds, chapters, music beds, loudness with the room taken down, another camera of a take placed by hand, and
now speakers found from their voices.

1. **Cameras that follow whoever is talking.** Speakers found after the transcript and matched to the people on
   screen and to the cameras (Cutalong already watches mouths). In an episode: a switch within about a second of each
   new voice, shots held a median 10 s, about a third of switches 1 to 3 s on the listener, the wide shot as a reset,
   and a pace to choose (about 2 to 17 switches a minute). In a clip: full frame on the speaker, a cut every 1.3 to 3 s.
2. **A hook builder.** For an episode, a cold open from the transcript: the most striking claims, numbers, the taboo,
   question-and-answer pairs and a question left hanging, 3 to 13 bites of 3 to 8 s (30 to 95 s in all), rising, ending
   on the cliffhanger, music under it, white flashes on a few cuts; or one teaser moment; or none, starting on the best
   stretch. For a clip, start on the first word of the question or claim, the payoff inside 3 s. (Cutalong's vlogs
   already tease their strongest moment first.)
3. **The opening around it:** tease, a 2 to 6 s sting, an optional ask, talking within about 45 s; a sting and corner
   logo made from the brand, in a few styles (fade, slide, letters one by one, typed, glitch).
4. **The body left alone.** Keep it as said: no jump cuts, pauses kept (Cutalong's podcast cut already does this), at
   most dead air trimmed under a camera switch.
5. **A headline card on clips,** on from the first frame for 2 to 3 s ("HOW TO SPOT A PSYCHOPATH IN 5 SECONDS",
   "[NAME] ON [TOPIC]"), including Colin & Samir's black on yellow boxes.
6. **Names and evidence on screen:** the guest's name the first time they are seen; screenshots of articles with a
   highlighter wipe and a slow push, charts drawing on, a page with the speaker in a box, a source label; in clips,
   what is named shown as it is named, for 0.3 to 2 s.
7. **Captions where they belong:** on clips, 1 to 3 words swapping on the word, bold with an edge, 40 to 62% down,
   coloured by keyword or speaker; in an episode, none in the body and keywords on the word only in the cold open.
   Text appears with a cut, a fade or a slide; nothing needs to bounce.
8. **Stacked clips:** speaker over footage, or host over guest, with a thin divider.
9. **Sound:** music only under the hook, the sting and the outro; voices cleaned and gated between words (the room
   clean built for sermons is most of this).
10. **Packaging:** chapters with the sponsor reads marked, an outro pointing to the next video, and endings on the
    punchline or the laugh.
11. **Choosing the model.** Cutalong always uses Claude Opus 5.5 to edit and to pick clips, and Settings offers no
    other Claude model (only a model on the computer, under Local AI). For a podcast the clip finder reads the whole
    1 to 3 hour transcript, so the model decides how long that takes and what it costs: a choice in Settings (Opus for
    the best picks, a faster, cheaper model for quick passes) may matter more here than anywhere.
12. **Zooms, sparingly, and only on clips.** No episode in the 21 zoomed, and 4 of 7 clips measured didn't either. Where
    clips do, a slow push of 1.1 to 1.3x on the face over 1 to 3 s, or a snap at a cut. Cutalong almost never zooms
    today (only on a jump cut an edit makes, or when asked, and never on a picture already cropped close), which is
    nearly right for podcasts; on clips it could offer a push now and then.
