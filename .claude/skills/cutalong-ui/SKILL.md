# Building Cutalong's interface

Cutalong is a video editor people talk to. Its direction is **Lined Script, on set, quiet by default**: the transcript is the edit, so the app reads like a shooting script. Paper on a softly lit desk, generous air, replies set beautifully in a serif, versions as revision pages. The footage is the picture: real stills and real playback, sitting on **the stage**, a dark surface in both themes, with the tools that act on it. Words stay on paper. Dark mode is a calm night page, never a void.

**Quiet by default (2026-11-21, the owner's "unslop").** No mascots or characters, no orbs or blobs, no stickers, no confetti of pastel blocks. Colour appears only where it means something: a version's revision colour (its tab, its pages), the stage under footage, coral for the cut, and a brand's own colour as a dot. Everything else is paper and hairlines. One highlighter per moment, on Start and on the welcome only. References, through Uizze: Kobbe, Granola, Linear and Superlist for the restraint; Higgsfield and Runway for real thumbnails; the workspace settings pages for a plain avatar and name. Ask "is this the product, or decoration?" of anything new, and cut the decoration.

The design system is published at https://claude.ai/artifact/AsZpkLeXa7pLaqMZkipqLh; `src/renderer/src/cutalong/tokens.json` is its copy, and `tokens.test.ts` fails if `tokens.css` drifts from it, in either theme. The thinking behind the direction is in `docs/design/README.md`, and the clickable reference is `docs/design/prototype/index.html` (Lined Script in its picker).

## Rules

1. **One thing per screen.** Start is a question and a composer. A thread is a reply and its buttons. The editor is a preview, the notes or the script, and the beat strip. If a second thing wants the same screen, it goes behind a button, a menu or Details.
2. **Tokens only.** Every colour, space, radius, shadow, duration and curve comes from `tokens.css` (light and dark), or reuses a rule already in `cutalong.css`. Components carry no colour values of their own. The only literals are footage colours on the picture itself (dips to black or white, caption white) and the gloss on a dropped still.
3. **Paper, ink, soft greys, and the stage.** `--ground` is the desk (with `--glow`, a soft light from above), `--card` and `--card-2` are paper, `--well` is where footage and your own words sit, `--line`, `--line-mid` and `--line-strong` are hairlines, button edges, and the edges of fields and the composer (3:1). The composer is lifted paper: a field's `--line-strong` edge and a soft shadow; focused, its edge firms and a halo of blue pencil shows, never a heavy ring. **The stage** (`--stage`, `--stage-2`, `--stage-line`, `--on-stage`, `--on-stage-soft`, `--radius-stage`, `--shadow-stage`) holds footage and what acts on it: the editor's preview (`.cl-well`), the beat strip (`.cl-strip`), project stills, sample shoots, the welcome's demo. Put `.cl-stage` on anything else that belongs there. The stage is a scope as well as a surface: inside it `--ink`, `--line`, `--card` and friends point at the stage's tokens, so a button, a beat or a ring set on it needs no rules of its own.
4. **Coral is the cut.** The playhead and the mark's dashed bar. Nothing else: never text, never a fill, never a second coral thing in one place. The assistant's tile carries the mark beside the newest reply only; while it thinks, the mark breathes.
5. **The revision colours come three ways.** Use `revisionOf(n)` (`revisions.ts`) for versions and `block()`/`bloom()` (`shapes.ts`) for the rest; never pick a tint by hand.
   - `--rev-1` to `--rev-7` (white, blue, pink, yellow, green, goldenrod, salmon): gentle page tints behind the script and the strip for the version on screen (the editor sets `--rev`).
   - `--block-*` (blue, pink, yellow, green, gold, salmon, lavender): a version's tab and the highlighter. Not section, card or tile backgrounds: facts, presets, clips, a brand's hero, onboarding panels and empty pages are paper (`--card` with a `--line` hairline). After dark they are quiet night tints (`tokens.test.ts` holds them there).
   - `--bloom-*`: the deeper tone of each, for a brand's colour: its dot (`Blob`, now a circle) and its avatar (a circle with its initials). Text on a bloom is `--on-bloom`, dark in both themes.
6. **Blue pencil picks.** `--pick` marks a stretch picked on the strip and the script lines it covers.
7. **One primary button per view** (ink fill; on the stage the scope makes it paper-filled). Everything else is secondary (paper with a `--line-mid` edge) or text. In a thread only the newest reply keeps its primary; older ones step down on their own. In the editor, Export steps down to secondary while notes wait to be applied, so applying them is the one primary.
8. **Type with a voice.** Display headings are big, bold and tight (Figtree 800: onboarding 96/92 and 72/70, Start 68/68, pages 40/44). Only Start ("What are we *making*?") and the welcome ("Talk to your *footage*") carry an accent word: the serif's italic on a highlighter swipe (`<span className="cl-accent">`, a typographic voice rather than stress, so not `<em>`; the swipe is `--hl`, yellow unless the version or step gives another block colour). Never accent a name someone typed, and never a page title. **Source Serif 4** for what is read: replies (18/29, at most 36em, `text-wrap: pretty`), the script (16.5/27), notes (italic, pencilled), the plain line under long work. Small quiet labels beside the big type. JetBrains Mono only for commands, timecode and file names. Figures are tabular wherever they count or tick. Fonts are bundled Latin subsets (`fonts.css`, `fonts/`, licences beside them): the content security policy loads fonts only from the app.
9. **Plain shapes.** Pills for buttons, chips and segmented controls; `--radius-stage` for the stage; `--radius-card` for sheets; `--radius-thumb` for posters and Details; `--radius-composer` for the composer. No organic shapes: a brand is a dot of its colour (`Blob`, drawn as a circle) and its avatar a circle with its initials (`BrandAvatar`); "new" on a version made while you watched is a small word beside its tab, not a sticker. Shadows are soft light: `--shadow-page`, `--shadow-float`, `--shadow-composer`, `--shadow-frame`, `--shadow-tab`.
10. **Big numbers, small labels.** A plain sentence first, then at most three facts (`Facts` in `Message.tsx`, `factParts()` in `facts.ts`) on paper, not coloured tiles: a big number with a label under it, or a short phrase when there is no number. The rest lives behind Details.
11. **States say so three ways.** Disabled: a paler fill, muted words, the not-allowed cursor. Errors: `--error` on the field's edge and the message, with an icon (`.cl-field-msg.error`); success in `--ok`. Focus is a soft halo, a 4 px outline of blue pencil at 18% that shows at once, with the field's edge firming to ink; buttons keep their 2 px ink ring.
12. **Hover needs a pointer.** Hover rules live inside `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`.

## First run

- **Onboarding** (`screens/Onboarding.tsx`) is one step a screen, with no rail: Welcome (the cut shown on the stage, not footage faked), Account, the Anthropic key, your channel (its name and avatar, saved to the brand), and a few of your videos to learn from. Step dots at the top right; Back and the step's one primary at the foot. The account step is drawn but not wired, so it shows only in the browser mock until accounts exist. It runs once (`firstRun.ts`), decided when the launch check answers: someone already editing never sees it.
- **The setup card** sits at the foot of the open rail until every step is done (`setupSteps()`: the key, the channel's name, a first edit, one export). Each step left is a button to where it happens.
- **Start** shows the presets as pastel cards that show what they do (`PRESETS` in `Start.tsx`), then your projects on the stage, or, with none yet, sample shoots to try a first cut on.
- The rail is open by default where there is room (1100 px and up) and remembers how it was left.

## Work in progress, and what Cutalong learns

- **Long work** (`Working` with `big`) is a checklist of the steps it has taken, in the app's own words: the step under way in ink with a pulsing dot ("Transcribing take-3.mov"), the ones before ticked and in the past ("Transcribed take-3.mov"), then one plain sentence, the thin progress line and the time. No characters, no orb. Progress is real progress: a stage the app does not report is never drawn.
- **Replies** carry no credits line. Notes say where they are, not who takes them.
- **What I've learned** (`screens/Crew.tsx`, "Learned" in the rail) is one sheet, one row per kind of work (Sound, The cut, Words on screen, Finding things, The look, Getting it out): what it covers, what has been learned about how you like it (`crewLearned`), and what it still needs. `crew.ts` still names the jobs internally (for grouping stages and learned facts); those names never reach the screen.

## Components to reach for

- **Revision tabs** (`Revisions` in `Editor.tsx`): the newest versions as tabs in their revision colours; the one on screen is ringed in ink and keeps the `cl-version` class, whose text is only `v{n}`. A version made while the editor is open has a small "new" beside its tab.
- **The word track** (`WordTrack`, on a phone or a window under 640 px): the edit's words as chips on the stage, just above the beat strip. Tap a word to jump to it; hold one (or Shift-click) to mark it, then tap more; one button cuts them or brings struck ones back (`toggleOps`, shared with the script). Struck words stay, crossed out in coral; the playing word is ringed; the row follows the playhead unless it was just moved.
- **The script** (`ScriptPanel`): numbered lines with a coverage line, an asterisk on every line the revision changed (`changedBeats()`), open notes pencilled in the margin.
- **A brand's avatar** (`BrandAvatar`, `avatarOf(brand)`): a circle in the colour picked on the Brand page (`AvatarPicker`, colours only), with the brand's initials; in the brand switch and the brand list, a dot of that colour.
- **Composer files**: round, glossy stills that float up into the composer (`.cl-attachment` with its `.cl-poster`).
- **Fields and steps for key setup**: `.cl-field` with `aria-invalid`, `.cl-field-msg` (`.error`, `.ok`), and `.cl-steps` (`.now` for the step that fixes what went wrong, `.done` for the ones a key Anthropic accepted shows are done). `KeySteps` draws any service's steps from `shared/setup`, and `ClaudeKeyField` is the Anthropic key's field: Start shows them until a key works (`KeySetup`), and Settings' Anthropic key row opens them while one does not.
- **Toasts** float at the top right under the header, on paper, and never cover the beat strip.
- **Settings** (`screens/Settings.tsx`, sections in `screens/settings/`) is a list of sections beside one section at a time, as Claude's own settings are: Claude, Local AI, Services | Words, B-roll, Made videos | This Mac (and Licences on a phone). Each section's mark is its line icon on a pastel tile (`block()`). A setting is a `SetRow` (`settings/rows.tsx`): a title, at most a tag (`.cl-set-tag`: "Connected", "Paid", "Offline"), one or two lines of what it does or how it stands, and its control at the end: `SetSwitch`, `SetSelect` (a pill menu, for more than three choices), `SetSegments` (three or fewer, which sit under the words) or a small button. What needs room (a key's steps, a list of folders) opens under its row. Rows sit on paper cards (`SetGroup`, with a small heading when a section has more than one). Every key besides Anthropic's is under Services, the AI services behind their one switch and the stock sites after them; other sections point there with an "Open Services" button rather than holding keys of their own. Search finds any row by its words, under its section. Never put a paragraph under a switch: the long story goes where it is needed (a key's terms under its field once it is opened, a quote in the chat).

## Motion

Things float in and settle; what lands, lands on a spring with a hair of overshoot; only work in progress moves on its own. Styles are in `motion.css`, the few pieces that need script in `motion.tsx`, and every value is a token.

- **How fast.** `--duration-fast` 120 ms for hover, press, focus and leaving; `--duration-base` 180 ms for menus and toggles; `--duration-gentle` 260 ms for arrivals; `--duration-calm` 340 ms for screens and shared elements; `--duration-spring` 460 ms for a spring's whole run.
- **Which curve.** Arrive on `--ease-enter`. What lands (a card, a fact, footage dropped in) lands on `--spring-settle` (4% overshoot); what pops (a setup step's tick) on `--spring-pop` (6%). Leave on `--ease-exit`, shorter, and never bounce. The highlighter draws across its word (a `scale` on `.cl-accent::before`, 140 ms after the heading). Move between two places on `--ease-standard`. Loops that breathe (the assistant's mark while it thinks, the dot of the step under way) use `--ease-breathe`, the one curve that eases in.
- **What moves.** A reply rises 8 px and comes into focus from a 2 px blur, then its parts follow at 50, 90, 120 and 140 ms, its tiles at 60, 100 and 140 ms. Clip cards, preset cards, project stills and sample shoots overlap at 0, 45 and 80 ms. An onboarding step's parts rise at 0, 50, 90 and 120 ms and its side panel lands at 120 ms. A setup step done pops its tick on `--spring-pop`; the card's bar grows on `--spring-settle`. Dropped stills float up 24 px, 40 ms apart within each drop, while the composer opens to hold them as one box (a View Transition, "attach", and "detach" when the last one is taken out: its edge drawn on the moving group, one picture inside kept to the bottom edge where the words and buttons are); a second drop only floats its stills in, and under reduced motion the stills start part-visible since the composer makes room at once. A brand's avatar takes a new colour on the base duration. While work runs, only the assistant's mark breathes and the dot of the step under way pulses. Anything that will count or tick holds its room from the start (the stopwatch is there, hidden, for its first second), so nothing shifts when it begins.
- **What appears at once.** Anything the keyboard opened. A list that arrives with its screen gets no second wave.
- **What never moves.** The footage, text being read, the layout (only transform, opacity and filter animate), anything idle, and coral.
- **Building it.** A new thing arriving takes an `@starting-style` transition in `motion.css`. Remove a row with `collapse()`, let something fall away with `useLingering()`, fill progress with `grow()`, change a screen with `viewTransition()`, and when something changes size in a way the layout would jump, give it a `viewTransition()` kind of its own (as the composer's "attach" and "detach") rather than animating height.
- **Reduced motion.** `--cl-travel` drops to 0: opacity still changes, nothing travels, scales, blurs or springs. The mark and the step's dot hold still.

## Voice

A helpful editor sitting next to you: first person, plain words, exact numbers. "v3 is ready. The hook word is bigger and the ending stays on you." Progress says the stage the app reports, plainly ("Transcribing take-3.mov", then "Transcribed take-3.mov"). No exclamation marks, no emoji, no praise, and never the word clone. Labels are verbs that say what happens. Seconds read as `0.15 s` (use `seconds()`), running times as `0:52` (use `clock()`).

## Where things are

- `App.tsx`: the rail and one screen at a time. `useCutalong.ts` holds the session.
- `components/`: `ui.tsx` (Mark, Button, IconButton, icons, AssistantAvatar), `Shapes.tsx` (Blob, now a dot; BrandAvatar, a circle with initials; Sticker and LivingMark, no longer used), `Crew.tsx` (no longer drawn), `Working` (the checklist), `Composer`, `Message` (with `Facts`), `BeatStrip`, `Sidebar`, `ClipSet`, `BrandSwitch`.
- `screens/`: Onboarding, Start, Thread, Editor, Brand and BrandRules, Library, Crew (What I've learned), and Settings with its sections in `screens/settings/`. The browser mock's posters and playback are real footage (`public/mock/`, `public/sample.mp4`), never painted stand-ins.
- `firstRun.ts`: whether onboarding ran, the first export, and the setup card's steps.
- `tokens.css` and `tokens.json`, `fonts.css` and `fonts/`, `revisions.ts`, `shapes.ts`, `crew.ts`, `facts.ts`, `working.ts`.
- `api.ts`: the slice of the preload API Cutalong uses. Add a method there and to `mock.ts` together.

## Checking a change

Heavy commands (typecheck, a full test run, a build, any app run) go through the shared lock: `flock /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-chop-shop/53b389d3-4ba3-5f34-8abd-cb88ea18110d/scratchpad/heavy.lock -c '<command>'`.

1. `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.
2. `npx electron-vite build`, then serve `out/renderer`. In a plain browser `api.ts` uses the mock, so every screen can be reached.
3. Drive it with Playwright at 1440 x 900, 1280 x 840, 960 x 640 and a phone's 390 x 844, light and dark, and look at the screenshots. Catch long work mid-checklist (drop a file on the composer), a reply, the editor playing, What I've learned, and the Brand page's picker.
4. Run Hallmark's lint and Impeccable's detector live on each screen, and the impeccable finish and motion reviewers, and fix what they find.
5. Ask of every screenshot: is anything on it decoration rather than the product? Quiet paper, real footage on the stage, colour only where it means something, beautifully set, and moving only where work is happening.
